Commissioned for ArtSqr

A monumental line, folded into home

Commissioned from the Italian muralist Millo, a single monumental line-drawing folds the inner courtyard into a private gallery. Known for his vast, graphic black-and-white figures set against pops of colour, Millo treats the façade as a canvas and the residents as its only audience.

Residents do not visit the work — they live beside it. The mural turns an everyday address into a piece of the city's cultural map.

The story

The art as part of the address

Public art usually belongs to everyone and no one. At ArtSqr the relationship is reversed: a single, permanent work is woven into the building itself, framed by the courtyard so that it becomes part of the daily rhythm of the residence rather than a destination to be sought out.

Millo's practice spans continents and façades many storeys tall. His figures are tender and oversized, quietly commenting on how people inhabit cities. Here, that sensibility is turned inward — toward the twenty-four families who share the courtyard.

The result is an address with a cultural signature: a home that is also a landmark, and a mural that ages alongside the people who live with it.
